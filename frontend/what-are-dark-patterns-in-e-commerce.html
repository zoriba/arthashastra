  • Dark patterns vs. honest persuasion:

    A legitimate persuasive design clearly presents options, risks, and benefits, whereas a dark pattern selectively hides or distorts this information, thereby undermining informed consent, autonomy, and fair choice.

  • Scope in digital commerce:

    These tactics appear across product pages, sign up forms, checkout flows, cookie banners, and subscription interfaces, often affecting pricing transparency, data consent, and post purchase control.

  • Prevalence in everyday shopping:

    Empirical studies report that large proportions of users face hidden charges, auto renewed subscriptions, and false urgency on mainstream e commerce platforms, indicating that dark patterns are not rare edge cases but systemic features of the digital marketplace.
