  • Loss aversion and fear of missing out:

    Prospect Theory suggests that people experience losses more intensely than equivalent gains, so threats of losing a discount, product, or limited time offer can push users into rapid, unreflective purchases.

  • Default and status quo bias:

    Many users accept pre set options simply because changing them requires additional attention and effort; this is why pre selected checkboxes and auto renewal settings are so powerful in shaping outcomes.

  • Endowment effect and sunk cost:

    Once users have invested time configuring a cart or exploring a service, they tend to overvalue that impending transaction and continue even after noticing hidden fees or problematic terms.

  • Cognitive load and decision fatigue:

    Complex interfaces, overloaded screens, and multiple micro decisions deplete mental resources, making users more vulnerable to defaults, urgency cues, and simplified “recommended” options.

  • Social proof and conformity:

    Seeing (or believing) that many others have bought, viewed, or endorsed a product makes individuals more likely to imitate that behaviour, especially under uncertainty or time pressure.
