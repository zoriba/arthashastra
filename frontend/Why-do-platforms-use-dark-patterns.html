  • Behavioural manipulation for data and consent:

    Interfaces are often structured to push users into sharing personal data or agreeing to broad terms—such as tracking cookies or marketing communications—by making privacy friendly choices difficult to find or cognitively costly.

  • Meeting performance and growth targets:

    In hyper competitive digital markets, internal pressure to show rising conversion rates, click throughs, and subscription numbers can incentivize design teams to cross the line from persuasive design into deceptive pattern deployment.

  • Competitive advantage and lock in:

    Practices like complex cancellation flows or “roach motel” subscription traps can reduce churn artificially, making user bases appear more stable while constraining genuine free exit and switching behaviour.
